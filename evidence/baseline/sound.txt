=== NUMBERED PASSAGE / பாடப்பகுதி ===
[S1] Sound is a form of mechanical energy that propagates through a material medium in the form of longitudinal waves produced by vibrating objects.
[S2] When a vibrating body oscillates back and forth, it compresses the surrounding air particles to generate a region of high pressure called a compression, followed by a region of low particle density known as a rarefaction.
[S3] These successive alternating compressions and rarefactions travel through elastic media such as air, water, or solid steel, transferring energy without the permanent physical displacement of the medium particles themselves.
[S4] Because sound propagation requires physical particle collisions to transmit acoustic disturbances, sound cannot travel across an empty vacuum.
[S5] The human ear detects these pressure oscillations through the vibration of the tympanic membrane, which transmits mechanical impulses through three delicate auditory ossicles to the cochlea inside the inner ear.
[S6] Frequency determines the characteristic pitch of a musical sound, whereas wave amplitude determines its perceived acoustic loudness and intensity.
