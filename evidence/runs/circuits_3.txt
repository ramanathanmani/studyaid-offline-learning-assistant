=== NUMBERED PASSAGE / பாடப்பகுதி ===
[S1] An electric current is defined as the rate of flow of electric charges across any cross section of a conductor.
[S2] In metallic wires, electrons constitute the flow of electric charges because free electrons drift when an electric potential difference is maintained by a chemical cell or battery.
[S3] By convention, the direction of electric current is considered opposite to the direction of the flow of electrons, moving from the positive terminal towards the negative terminal.
[S4] The continuous and closed conducting path along which an electric current flows is called an electric circuit.
[S5] When a circuit is broken or a switch is turned off, the current stops flowing instantly.
[S6] The electrical potential difference between two points is measured in volts using an instrument called a voltmeter, which is connected in parallel across the electrical component.
[S7] According to Ohm's law, the electric current passing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across its terminals, provided the temperature remains constant.
[S8] Electric resistance measures the opposition offered by a conductor to the flow of electric current.
