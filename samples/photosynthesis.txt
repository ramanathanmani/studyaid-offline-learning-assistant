Photosynthesis is the essential biological process by which green plants, algae, and certain bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy. During this biochemical reaction, plants absorb sunlight using the green pigment known as chlorophyll present inside cellular organelles called chloroplasts. Carbon dioxide from the atmospheric air enters the leaves through microscopic pores called stomata, while roots absorb water and dissolved minerals from the moist soil. Using the captured solar energy, water molecules are split into hydrogen and oxygen through photolysis. The hydrogen combines with carbon dioxide across enzymatic cycles to synthesize glucose, a simple carbohydrate that fuels plant respiration and vegetative growth. Oxygen is released as a vital byproduct into the atmosphere, sustaining aerobic life across terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems. Without photosynthesis, the carbon cycle would collapse, oxygen replenishment would cease, and food webs would disintegrate completely. In agricultural systems, environmental factors such as sunlight intensity, temperature variations, and moisture availability determine the overall rate of photosynthesis and crop productivity.
