Sound is a form of mechanical energy that propagates through a material medium in the form of longitudinal waves produced by vibrating objects. When a vibrating body oscillates back and forth, it compresses the surrounding air particles to generate a region of high pressure called a compression, followed by a region of low particle density known as a rarefaction. These successive alternating compressions and rarefactions travel through elastic media such as air, water, or solid steel, transferring energy without the permanent physical displacement of the medium particles themselves. Because sound propagation requires physical particle collisions to transmit acoustic disturbances, sound cannot travel across an empty vacuum. The human ear detects these pressure oscillations through the vibration of the tympanic membrane, which transmits mechanical impulses through three delicate auditory ossicles to the cochlea inside the inner ear. Frequency determines the characteristic pitch of a musical sound, whereas wave amplitude determines its perceived acoustic loudness and intensity.
